# Getting Started: How to Run a Season with LigiOpen

This page is a practical onboarding guide for league operators, county associations, and club leaders.

## Before you start

Agree on four basics:

- season window (start and end)
- competition structure (league only, cup only, or both)
- governance roles (who decides what)
- communication rhythm (how changes are announced)

Without this alignment, even the best software cannot protect fairness.

## Step 1: Open the season

Create your season and make it current.

Example: `2026/27`.

Why this matters: all standings, fixtures, and progression outcomes should be tied to the same season context.

## Step 2: Set up league hierarchy

Prepare your competition structure:

- league identity (for example national premier league)
- division layers (top tier, second tier, youth, women, etc. as needed)

This creates a clean operating map for everyone.

## Step 3: Team participation readiness

Invite clubs to submit participation.

League admins review and confirm legitimate entries.

This is where integrity begins: the right teams in the right divisions before fixture release.

## Step 4: Build club and squad readiness

Each club should have:

- active club admins
- organized team records
- player roster clarity
- realistic availability communication

This reduces matchday surprises.

## Step 5: Prepare venues and officials

For each competition window, confirm:

- venue readiness and blackout risks
- official availability and assignment depth
- county-level logistics that can affect kickoff timing

## Step 6: Generate fixtures with realism

Create fixtures and review them before public release.

A good fixture calendar respects travel, turnaround time, and venue practicality.

## Step 7: Publish and run match weeks

Once published, match information becomes public expectation.

Keep updates disciplined. If disruptions happen, communicate early and consistently.

## Step 8: Validate outcomes and keep trust

Match outcomes must be reviewed and made defensible.

Disputes should be structured, not emotional. Standings should be transparent, not mysterious.

## Step 9: Close and transition

At season close:

- confirm final outcomes
- communicate progression consequences
- capture lessons for the next cycle

## Kenyan readiness checklist (weekly)

- Any forecasted heavy rain in key venues?
- Any national/county event affecting transport or security?
- Any high-risk derby requiring extra control?
- Any critical officiating gaps?
- Any club reporting unusual availability constraints?

When this checklist is routine, operations quality improves quickly.
